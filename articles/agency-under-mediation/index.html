The common grammar

What remains after mediation?

Across institutions, professions, movements, churches, and machines, mediation begins by answering a limitation. The decisive question is whether the intervention returns a more capable subject—or quietly relocates authorship, judgment, and power into the mediator.

Mediation is not the enemy. It can add knowledge, coordination, protection, representation, care, and technical capability. But a successful outcome alone does not settle whether the mediated person remains the actor.

After mediation

The diagnostic that remains

Across all ten domains, the working invariant is stable: good mediation expands or preserves real capability while keeping authority, goal ownership, and authorship with the legitimate principal—except where a bounded and independently justified transfer exists.

  1. Who defines the goal, and who remains the subject of the action?
  2. What limitation is the mediator actually answering?
  3. Does the transformation build capability or deepen dependence?
  4. Where did the mediator's authority come from, and what bounds its scope?
  5. Can the mediated person contest, revise, refuse, or exit the arrangement meaningfully?
  6. After State₁, is the person more capable—or is the mediator more necessary?
